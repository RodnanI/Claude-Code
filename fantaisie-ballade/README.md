# Fantaisie-Ballade in D minor

*à la mémoire de W. A. Mozart, F. Chopin et F. Liszt*

An original five-minute piano piece that joins three styles in one arc: Mozart's
fantasia and classical grace, Chopin's ballade, and Liszt's virtuosity and
thematic transformation. The score is engraved with LilyPond. The recording is
rendered with the Salamander Grand Piano V3 samples, a Yamaha C5 with 16
velocity layers, played by a humanised performance model and placed in a
simulated concert hall.

## The music

The whole piece grows from one motif: **A - F - E - D**, a rising minor sixth
that falls back by step to the tonic. It is the opening motto, the ballade
melody, the head of the Mozartean theme (as C - A - G - F in F major), the bass
line of the storm and, in D major, the last thing you hear.

| Time | Bars | Section |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00 | 1-4 | **Grave.** The motto in bare octaves, answered by a hushed diminished seventh; the same again a semitone higher, on the Neapolitan (E-flat), crashing onto A7(b9) over the lowest A on the keyboard. |
| 0:30 | 5-10 | **Andante misterioso.** Rolling arpeggios in the manner of Mozart's D minor Fantasia K. 397. The bass falls chromatically D - C# - C - B - B-flat - A (lament bass: i, V6, i4/2, IV6, German sixth, V) while the top line climbs D - E - F - G - G# - A. |
| 0:55 | 11 | **Cadenza** *a piacere*: a diminished-seventh flourish to G6 and a cascade through the harmonic minor. |
| 1:05 | 11-19 | **Andante con moto, 6/8.** The ballade theme, *cantabile, con dolore*, over nocturne broken chords. Its leaps widen phrase by phrase (sixth, seventh, octave), and the consequent reaches a minor ninth up to B-flat over a Neapolitan sixth. |
| 1:27 | 20-27 | *Sotto voce* episode in B-flat major. It pivots on E-flat (IV of B-flat, but the Neapolitan of D minor) to swing back to the dominant. |
| 1:48 | 28-37 | The theme varied: sixteenth-note left hand, Chopin-style turns, then in octaves up to an *ff* climax and a deceptive cadence, *calando* onto C7. |
| 2:15 | 38-57 | **Allegretto grazioso, F major, 2/4.** A Mozart theme with Alberti bass, a dominant-pedal middle phrase, and a varied reprise with chromatic neighbour notes and written-out cadential trills. |
| 2:41 | 58-64 | **Minore.** The same theme in D minor over a *Sturm und Drang* murky bass. |
| 2:50 | 65-87 | **Allegro agitato e tempestoso.** The ballade theme in left-hand octaves under right-hand waves and tremolos. A Lisztian cycle of minor thirds (D - F - G# - B - D) follows, with the hands swapping roles. Over a dominant pedal the top voice climbs through alternating 6/4 and diminished-seventh chords to *fff*. Then comes a diminished-seventh cascade across the whole keyboard down to A0. |
| 3:26 | 88-100 | **Grandioso, D major.** The ballade theme transfigured in full chords over harp-like left-hand arpeggios, with a flattened-sixth (B-flat) climax and a *largamente* close (IV - iv - I6/4 - V7 - I). |

## How it was made

1. **Composition** (`compose.py`). Every bar is written by hand as LilyPond
   notation: right hand, left hand, dynamics and pedalling. Small helpers
   generate repeated figurations (cross-staff arpeggios, waves, tremolos,
   written-out trills). The script emits the `.ly` file plus a JSON plan of
   dynamics, pedal changes, tempo, rubato, fermatas and voicing.
2. **Engraving and score MIDI.** LilyPond 2.24 engraves the PDF and writes a
   metronomic MIDI with one track per hand. Accents come through as velocity.
3. **Performance** (`perform.py`). This turns the score into a human
   performance:
   - section tempi with phrase-level rubato: pushing through the middle of a
     phrase and broadening at its end
   - ritardandi and timed fermatas
   - dynamics and hairpins as continuous loudness curves
   - voicing: the melody stands out, inner chord tones are softer, and
     left-hand bass notes are weighted
   - metric accents and melodic-contour shaping
   - melody lead of about 14 ms
   - rolled wide chords and arpeggiated final chords
   - legato overlap
   - pedalling with a realistic catch after each change
   - small random deviations
   - a check that the two hands never collide on the same key
4. **Rendering.** [sfizz](https://github.com/sfztools/sfizz) (`sfizz_render`)
   plays the performance MIDI through the
   [Salamander Grand Piano V3 SFZ](https://github.com/sfzinstruments/SalamanderGrandPiano):
   48 kHz / 24-bit samples, 16 velocity layers, release samples, string
   resonance and pedal noise.
5. **Hall and mastering** (`master.py`). The dry signal is convolved with a
   synthetic stereo concert-hall impulse response: early reflections, then a
   late tail whose decay runs from 2.5 s in the bass to 0.5 s at the top.
   After that come gentle EQ, a slow leveler (1.5:1), a look-ahead peak
   limiter, and normalisation to -1 dBTP. The result is about -19 LUFS
   integrated with roughly 19 dB of dynamic range.

## Rebuilding

```sh
# tools: python3 with mido numpy scipy soundfile, LilyPond >= 2.24, ffmpeg
git clone --depth 1 https://github.com/sfzinstruments/SalamanderGrandPiano
git clone --depth 1 --recursive https://github.com/sfztools/sfizz
cmake -S sfizz -B sfizz/build -DCMAKE_BUILD_TYPE=Release -DSFIZZ_JACK=OFF -DSFIZZ_RENDER=ON -DSFIZZ_SHARED=OFF
cmake --build sfizz/build --target sfizz_render -j

SFZ="$PWD/SalamanderGrandPiano/Salamander Grand Piano V3.sfz" \
SFIZZ="$PWD/sfizz/build/library/bin/sfizz_render" \
./build.sh
```

`perform.py` takes an optional seed as a fourth argument. Different seeds give
different, equally valid takes.
