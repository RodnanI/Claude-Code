The roles

Data scientist

Questions to answers

Analysis, experiments, statistical modeling, classic ML, communicating results to decision makers. Heavy on SQL, statistics and judgment.

ML engineer

Models into products

Builds training pipelines, deploys and scales models, owns reliability. Strong software engineering plus solid ML.

AI engineer

Applications on foundation models

Builds products on LLMs: prompting, retrieval, evaluation, agents, cost and latency. The fastest-growing role right now.

MLOps / platform

Infrastructure for ML

Feature stores, orchestration, serving, monitoring, GPU clusters. Mostly DevOps and cloud skills, applied to ML.

Data engineer

The data under everything

Pipelines, warehouses, data quality. Not modeling, but every ML team depends on this work.

Research scientist

New methods

Invents and publishes new techniques. Usually expects a PhD or an equivalent research track record, and strong math.

Portfolio projects that impress

Hiring managers see endless Titanic and MNIST notebooks. Stand out with projects that show judgment, not just code:

  • Your own data: collect or scrape a dataset about something you care about (sports, music, local housing, game telemetry). Collecting and cleaning is real-world skill.
  • End to end: framing, baseline, model, evaluation, error analysis, and a deployed demo or API.
  • An honest write-up: what failed, what you would do next, where the model should not be used.
  • A RAG assistant over a real document collection, with an evaluation set measuring answer quality.
  • A Kaggle competition where you reach a respectable rank and document what moved the score.
  • A reimplementation of a classic algorithm or paper from scratch, compared with the library version.

Consistency beats intensity: an hour a day for six months will take you further than a heroic weekend. Build things, finish them, and write about them. Read papers when a problem needs them, not to keep up with every headline. And never lose the habit this guide tried to build: form a guess, test it, look at the result honestly.
